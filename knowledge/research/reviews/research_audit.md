## Known-Answer Bank

**Question 1**: Why did Khrushchev deploy nuclear missiles to Cuba in 1962?
- Evidence anchors: Troy University historiographical study, Cambridge neo-Durkheimian analysis, Taubman's "folly" assessment, colleagues' post-overthrow indictment, Khrushchev's own "defensive" framing
- Expected answer direction: Multiple overlapping motives (defend Cuba, close nuclear gap, Berlin leverage, China competition) with inadequate risk assessment and no contingency planning
- Strength: High — well-evidenced from multiple scholarly sources with declassified Soviet materials

**Question 2**: What motivated the 1956 Secret Speech?
- Evidence anchors: Marxist study guide's "pre-emptive strike" analysis, speech's deliberate scope limitation, Malin notes, scholarly consensus on power-struggle context
- Expected answer direction: Mixed motives — genuine moral revulsion AND tactical power play to discredit Stalinist rivals. Not either/or.
- Strength: High — multiple converging lines of evidence

**Question 3**: Why was Khrushchev overthrown in 1964?
- Evidence anchors: Colleagues' "hare-brained schemes" indictment, party bifurcation alienating apparatchiks, agricultural failures, Cuban humiliation, contemporaneous NYT cables
- Expected answer direction: Policy failures (agriculture, industry) + decision-making style (erratic, unconsultative) + alienation of every major elite constituency
- Strength: High — well documented

## Edge-Case Candidate

**Question**: How would Khrushchev assess a contemporary leader making a high-risk territorial gamble that risks triggering a wider war (e.g., Putin's 2022 Ukraine invasion)?

- Why adjacent but under-evidenced: Khrushchev died in 1971 — he never saw post-Soviet Russia. But his own experience authoring high-risk territorial gambles (Berlin, Cuba), his Ukrainian experience, and his pattern of pragmatic retreat when costs escalate provide a framework for extrapolation.
- Expected reasoning approach: Would likely analyze through lenses of (1) buffer zone logic (which he understood deeply), (2) adversary resolve misreading (his own systematic bias), (3) domestic elite management (the key variable in his own survival), (4) the escalation → humiliation → domestic backlash cascade that destroyed him.
